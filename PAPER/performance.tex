\subsection{Latency, Throughput and Energy}
\label{sec:perf}

Area and power alone do not settle a comparison between a single-cycle
multiplier and a deep pipeline. Table~\ref{tab:perf} therefore reports
throughput, latency and energy per rotation, all derived from the same
synthesis runs: $F_{\max}$ from the slack against the 2\,ns constraint,
latency as pipeline depth divided by $F_{\max}$, and energy as the reported
power at the 500\,MHz operating point.

consumes $\sin/\cos$ it does not generate, and is therefore not a complete
RoPE unit.}
\label{tab:perf}

\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}

The two halves of the table tell opposite stories, and the reason is the
precision requirement established in \S\ref{sec:precision}. At $W=9$ a plain
multiplier is the cheapest option on every axis --- smaller, lower latency and
lowest energy --- and no amount of architectural argument changes that. But
$W=9$ is not a precision at which these models run: Qwen2-7B has already lost
$6.4\%$ perplexity there. At the word length the accuracy data actually
supports, the ordering inverts. Multiplier area grows as $O(W^2)$ while a
shift-add pipeline grows as $O(WN)$, so by $W=14$ the merged UD datapath is
$17\%$ smaller than the multiplier, $36\%$ smaller than the LUT, and lowest in
energy per rotation at $3.53$\,pJ. It is also the only design in that half of
the table with comfortable timing margin: the LUT closes with $26$\,ps of
slack at $W=14$ and fails outright at $W=16$.

The latency column is where the pipeline pays. At $W=14$ a rotation takes
$4.48$\,ns through the merged pipeline against $1.40$\,ns through the
multiplier. This matters far less than it appears, because RoPE is applied to
every $(q,k)$ pair independently: the design is throughput-bound, not
latency-bound, and one rotation is retired per cycle once the pipeline is
full. At $1.117$\,GHz a single lane sustains $1.12\times10^{9}$ rotations per
second, and the $d_h/2 = 64$ lanes of one attention head are independent.

\subsubsection{Cost in an attention pipeline}
A LLaMA-2-7B-class model applies RoPE to $d_h/2 = 64$ pairs, across $32$ heads,
for both $\mathbf{q}$ and $\mathbf{k}$, in each of $32$ layers --- $131\,072$
rotations per generated token. At the energies in Table~\ref{tab:perf} that is

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
\toprule
Design & Energy per token \\
\midrule
Merged UD ($N{=}5$) & \textbf{463\,nJ} \\
Multiplier$\dagger$ & 548\,nJ \\
Binary UD ($N{=}10$)& 734\,nJ \\
LUT                 & 1191\,nJ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

so the choice of rotation datapath is worth roughly $730$\,nJ per token
between the best and worst options here --- before accounting for the fact
that the multiplier row still owes the cost of producing $\sin$ and $\cos$.
